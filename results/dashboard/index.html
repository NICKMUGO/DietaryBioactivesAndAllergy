Reading the pathways

Up in allergy: interleukin signalling (IL-4/IL-13 targets), immunoregulatory interactions, granulocyte degranulation and a mitotic cell-cycle module. This is type-2 inflammation with epithelial hyperplasia.

Down in allergy: de novo ceramide synthesis (DEGS1/2, SPTLC3), bile-acid and sterol metabolism, nuclear receptors. Low ceramide is a hallmark of the atopic skin barrier, and the asthma GWAS gene ORMDL3 regulates the same pathway.

Sulforaphane's overlap is mostly mitotic cell-cycle genes from cancer studies, plus eicosanoid and redox genes. Only the second part is allergy-specific.

Limitations and next steps
  • CTD is curated literature: mostly cancer cell lines and high doses, dominated by quercetin and resveratrol. A missing enrichment does not prove a missing effect, and bioavailability is ignored.
  • Some disease changes are compensatory (NRF2), so "reversal" is a crude test of benefit.
  • The studies differ in tissue, platform and age, and two have 16 or fewer samples. GSE41861 nasal and bronchial samples share subjects, and their values had the sex signal removed.
  • Leaving out one large study changes 30–60% of signature genes, so read single genes as replicated leads, not a final list.
  • Next: score reversal with perturbation data (LINCS L1000) at realistic doses; follow sulforaphane, curcumin and EGCG in single-cell airway and skin data; test the ceramide barrier axis.
